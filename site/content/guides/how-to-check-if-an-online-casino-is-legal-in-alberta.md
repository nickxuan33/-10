Only sites approved by the Alberta iGaming Corporation (AiGC) are part of Alberta's regulated online gambling market. These five checks take a few minutes and tell you whether a site qualifies.

## 1. Find the site on AiGC's approved list

Open the [Approved iGaming Sites list](https://www.abigaming.ca/players/approved-igaming-sites) on AiGC's website and look for the exact brand. This is the check that matters most: AiGC says that any operator not in its directory is not regulated.

## 2. Compare the web address

Copycat sites sometimes use names and designs that look like well-known brands. Make sure the address in your browser matches the site on AiGC's list. Type the address yourself or follow the link from AiGC rather than a link in an ad or a message.

## 3. Look for the AiGC logo, then verify it

Approved operators display the AiGC logo. A logo is easy to copy, though, so treat it as a first signal and confirm the site with step 1.

## 4. Expect age, identity and location checks

Approved sites must confirm that you are at least 18 and that you are in Alberta. If a site lets you deposit without checking who you are or where you are, it is probably not regulated in Alberta.

## 5. Watch for warning signs

- Public ads that promise large bonuses or free play. Approved operators may not promote inducements like these in public advertising.
- Messages suggesting that gambling can help with money problems.
- A licence from another country presented as proof that the site is legal in Alberta.
- No way to set limits or to exclude yourself.

## If a site fails these checks

Don't deposit. If you already have an account on a site that is not approved, stop depositing and consider withdrawing your balance. Sites outside the regulated market are not covered by AiGC's complaints process, so problems with them can be hard to resolve.
