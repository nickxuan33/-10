**Short answer: yes.** Online gambling is legal in Alberta when you play at a site that the Alberta iGaming Corporation (AiGC) has approved. The province opened its regulated online market to private operators on July 13, 2026, and you must be 18 or older to play.

## How Alberta regulates online gambling

The regulated market is set up under the *iGaming Alberta Act*, which became law in 2025. Two provincial organizations share the work:

- **Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis (AGLC)** is the regulator. It registers operators, sets the standards they must meet, checks that they comply and runs the province-wide self-exclusion system.
- **The Alberta iGaming Corporation (AiGC)** is a Crown corporation that conducts and manages online gaming. It signs the commercial agreements with operators and handles complaints from the public.

An operator needs both an AGLC registration and an agreement with AiGC before it can take bets from players in Alberta.

## Which sites are approved?

AiGC publishes the official [list of approved iGaming sites](https://www.abigaming.ca/players/approved-igaming-sites). It is the only reliable way to confirm that a site is part of the regulated market, and AiGC's position is simple: an operator that is not in its directory is not regulated in Alberta.

The list changes as new operators launch, so check it again before you sign up anywhere. Our guide on [how to check whether an online casino is legal in Alberta](/guides/how-to-check-if-an-online-casino-is-legal-in-alberta/) walks you through it.

### What about PlayAlberta?

PlayAlberta, the province's own gambling site, is still available. It now operates alongside the private operators.

## What about sites licensed somewhere else?

Before the regulated market opened, many Albertans played on sites licensed in other countries. A licence from another jurisdiction does not make a site part of Alberta's regulated market. If you play on such a site, you are outside Alberta's player protections, including AiGC's complaints process and the province-wide self-exclusion system.

## What regulation means for players

Approved sites have to meet Alberta's standards. For you, that means:

- **Age and identity checks**, because every player must be 18 or older.
- **Location checks**, because approved sites may only take bets from people in Alberta.
- **Responsible gambling tools**, and a connection to the province-wide [self-exclusion program](https://gamesenseab.ca/responsible-gambling-support/self-exclusion-program).
- **A complaints process** through AiGC if you can't resolve a problem with the operator.

## Advertising rules to know about

Alberta limits how approved operators advertise. For example, they may not promote bonuses and other inducements in public advertising, and their ads must not present gambling as a solution to money problems. A loud public ad pushing a big bonus is a warning sign that the site may not be regulated in Alberta.
